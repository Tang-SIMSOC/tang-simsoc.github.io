**Negative influence** refers to the phenomenon in which people's opinions become more different after interaction. This contrasts with the classical assumption of assimilation, or positive influence, where interaction leads individuals to become more similar. Negative influence is one of the fundamental mechanisms that may explain polarization, although empirical evidence for its existence remains mixed.

In this project, we aim to find empirical evidence for negative influence. We apply **Stochastic Actor-Oriented Models (SAOMs)** to the longitudinal network data from **[The Arnhem School Study (TASS)](https://research.rug.nl/en/datasets/the-arnhem-school-study-tass-secondary-school-module/)**, which records adolescents' social networks and opinion dynamics. We find that negative influence can occur when the opinion distance between individuals is sufficiently large. For more information, see our recent [publication](https://www.jasss.org/28/1/2.html).
