I am a postdoctoral researcher at the [Computational Science Lab](https://uva.computationalscience.nl/) in the Informatics Institute at the University of Amsterdam. Before joining UvA, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the [Faculty of Behavioural and Social Sciences](https://www.rug.nl/gmw/?lang=en) at the University of Groningen. I completed my PhD at the [Faculty of Technology, Policy and Management](https://www.tudelft.nl/en/tpm) at TU Delft.

As a computational social scientist, my research examines how emergent social phenomena—including polarization, opinion diffusion, and network–opinion co-evolution—arise from interactions among individuals. I primarily use agent-based modeling and social network analysis, especially [Stochastic Actor-Oriented Models](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~snijders/siena/), with both synthetic and empirical network data. I also have experience teaching and supervising students in computational science and quantitative sociology.

I am currently studying the mechanisms of opinion polarization, including bounded confidence and negative influence. By integrating agent-based modeling with Stochastic Actor-Oriented Models, my collaborators and I aim to uncover the hidden sources of negative influence: what makes people move away from each other? See my latest publication in [JASSS](https://www.jasss.org/28/1/2.html), co-authored with [Andreas Flache](https://flache.gmw.rug.nl) and [Tom Snijders](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~snijders/).

I am also interested in statistical physics applications in social science. My work in this area includes opinion dynamics models based on the Ising model and Hopfield networks. The [Action-Opinion Inference Model (AOI)](https://www.jasss.org/22/3/2.html) is a representative example of this line of research. 
By combining insights from both statistical physics and social science, my work also contributes to multi-agent systems and social AI by designing, simulating, and analyzing intelligent systems of agent-agent and human-agent interactions. This is highlighted by my research on strategic [obfuscation](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0022250X.2021.1929968), which formalizes how highly autonomous agents dynamically minimize disclosed information in the AOI framework. The research can be applied to privacy protection in social media and human-AI communications.
